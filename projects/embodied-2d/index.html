How this works

Each body has 10 motorised joints (both legs and both arms are separate) and a hand that can close. Every 1/60 s a small neural network reads what the body senses (balance, joint angles, pressure under heels and toes, the ground ahead, its energy and tiredness, where the goal is) and sets a target for every motor.

Nobody programs the movements, and nothing copies a human: the ideal movement in Test is only there to compare. Training tries 48 slightly different brains, keeps the best and repeats. Difficulty grows as they improve: shoves, food farther away, sticks and logs, and the grip assist fades away.

Muscles are realistic-ish: pushing costs energy even when holding still, sustained effort tires a joint, and every attempt has a limited energy store. In Walk the food is far away, so a wasteful way of walking runs out before it gets there.

Test lets you move the limbs and see how the agent reacts. Switch the brain off to see the body go limp, or play the ideal movement: it takes over the body and moves every joint the way a person would, under the same physics, so you can see what the real body does with it. Nothing there affects learning.

New agents start knowing nothing. Kit, Juno and Pax are trained examples.
